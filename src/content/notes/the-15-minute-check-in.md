["What's actually in a health snapshot"](/notes/what-is-in-a-health-snapshot) explored what the judgement of a healthy service is based on (monitoring dashboards, hardware alarms, open tickets or even just a "feeling"). Here we explore where a "feeling" of the environment is useful because, when done deliberately, getting a feeling for the end-user experience is the most human health check there is.

Most evidence-gathering is a mix of automation and tailoring. Health snapshots collect results across the whole environment and roll them up so small indicators can be judged as a whole. However, there's one layer of health which automation can't quite reach - how it actually feels to use the service. Support requests tell you when enough people have been inconvenienced enough to report something but a deliberate look from the users' side can tell you first.

A micro report is a human inspection of the user experience on an HPC environment. It's a guided process engineered to fit inside 10-15 minutes and its whole purpose is to capture how the service *feels* on the day you run it.

### What the fifteen minutes buys you

**A view from the users' side.** By logging in through non-admin routes and doing what a user does (seeing login messages, checking scheduler's queue, using the tools they interact with). You're inspecting the service as it's consumed, not as it's installed.

**The unspectacular problems.** Finding slow, quiet changes that traditional automation won't detect is what can really impact end-users whether it's a queue behaving oddly, a tool that feels sluggish today, capacity that seems somehow less than last month. Nothing here raises an alarm. It takes a human noticing to catch a human-scale problem.

**A feeling for the environment, on the record.** One impression is an anecdote but repeated impressions are an insight. Writing it down each cycle turns opinion into evidence and lets you compare how it feels today against how it felt last month.

### Why guided matters

If left unchecked a human inspection can grow into an audit which becomes a rabbit hole and then a habit that quietly stops being kept. The guidance keeps the scope honest and precise (focusing on the things users actually touch) and keeps the clock honest too, so it stays repeatable. Ten minutes of looking properly beats an afternoon of looking occasionally.

### Where it fits

Health snapshots tell you what is true but the micro report captures what it's like to stand in it as a user. With hardware status and service availability around regular inspections this completes the layered picture of the environment's health.
