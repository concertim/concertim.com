Answering the question **"is your HPC & AI service healthy?"** is not straightforward. Is it based on monitoring dashboards? Hardware alarms? Number of open support tickets? A feeling? Some mix of them all? 

The health and status of hardware and the coverage of monitoring is one thing but it's only one layer of your health story. There are places that these methods often don't go; hardware/software configuration that has drifted from expectation, service behaviour that is undesired, the actual needs and experiences of your end-users.

A health snapshot is how we reach those layers. It's a structured compilation of insights and observations which we are going to unpack here to understand how it is more than a status page. 

### It starts with results

Collection of evidence and health metrics is the most important part. In modern systems it's easy to get caught up collecting "all the data" that seeing what needs attention gets buried behind a wave of alerts, red and orange statuses and other noise. Knowing what to look at and how to combine that with other insights and contextual information (like the role the host plays in the service) is the first step in creating a health snapshot.

### The roll-up

Small health indicators alone may not be something actionable. By combining, collating and judging the relationship of results to the HPC service as a whole it highlights what areas/aspects of your HPC service are worth looking at. Whether it be a group of identical compute nodes, the scheduler's capacity or environment access pathways - the highlighting of these areas gives a clearer overview of where efforts lay to improve system health.

### People are where results and knowledge collide

A key step of any health process is what only a human who knows the service can do. That is to filter, prioritise and action based on review of the provided health information. The importance of actionable items is a combination of multiple criteria that are unique to the specific service, things like: 

- The impact to **capacity or availability** of the service
- The current **end-user demand** on the service 
- The **comparative weight** against other actionable items 
- **Available time/resources** for taking action

### Recommendations based on best practice and experience

Health snapshot insights aren't just what needs looking at. Through our years of experience managing systems we have evolved many standardised processes for taking action to restore and improve service health. These processes are exposed alongside insights whether we're the ones taking action or you'd like to follow them yourself.

### How it answers "is your HPC & AI service healthy?"

All together the information and processes provide backing to the feeling that a service is healthy. By observing hardware status, comparing it to service availability and, ultimately, the experience of a typical end-user it is possible to build an accurate picture of your service health.
